# 40W CO2 Laser Cutter: Quick Usage Guide

> **Read this before you use the laser. If you're not sure about something, don't cut it. Ask first.**

---

## 🔥 1. Fire Safety: Never Leave the Laser Unattended

- **Stay with the machine the whole time it is running.** A small flame can turn into a fire inside the enclosure in seconds.
- Know where the **fire extinguisher** is (a CO2 or ABC type) and keep a **spray bottle of water** nearby for small flare-ups.
- **If a flame doesn't go out on its own:** pause or stop the job, keep the lid **closed** for a moment to starve it of air, then deal with it. Do not open the lid and blow air on it.
- Keep **air assist ON**. It helps stop flare-ups and keeps the lens clean.
- Keep **exhaust / ventilation ON** during the job and for a minute afterwards to clear the smoke.
- Clean scraps and debris out of the bed and the tray under the honeycomb regularly. Built-up debris is fuel.

---

## ☠️ 2. If You Smell CHLORINE: SHUT THE LASER DOWN IMMEDIATELY

A chlorine or "swimming pool" smell means the material has chlorine in it (almost always PVC or vinyl).
Burning it makes **chlorine gas and hydrochloric acid**. That is toxic to breathe, and it **corrodes the machine's mirrors, rails, and electronics**.

1. **Stop the job and turn the laser off.**
2. Leave the **exhaust running** and ventilate the room.
3. Remove the material once the smoke has cleared, and **do not cut it again**.

---

## 🧽 3. Foams: Safe vs. NOT Safe

| ✅ Generally SAFE | ❌ NOT SAFE: Do Not Cut |
|---|---|
| **Polyethylene (PE) foam** (packing foam, tool-drawer foam) | **PVC foam** (Sintra, Komatex, foam board with PVC): **chlorine gas** |
| **EVA foam** (craft foam, cosplay foam) *(ventilate well; it can flare)* | **Polyurethane (PU) foam** (upholstery/cushion foam, most sponges): gives off **hydrogen cyanide** |
| **Polypropylene (PP) foam** | **Polystyrene / Styrofoam / EPS**: catches fire very easily and drips burning plastic |
| | **Any foam you can't identify** |

---

## 🟫 4. Rubber: Safe vs. NOT Safe

| ✅ Generally SAFE | ❌ NOT SAFE: Do Not Cut |
|---|---|
| **"Laser-safe" / laser engraving rubber** (made for stamps) | **Neoprene** (polychloroprene): contains **chlorine** |
| **Natural rubber** (pure latex/gum rubber, no chlorine) | **Chlorinated rubbers / Hypalon** |
| **Silicone rubber** *(leaves white dust; ventilate)* | **Vinyl / PVC "rubber"** (mats, some gaskets) |
| | **Unknown black rubber** (tires, random gaskets, O-rings) |

---

## 🚫 5. Other Materials to NEVER Cut

- **PVC / Vinyl** (any kind): chlorine gas
- **Polycarbonate / Lexan**: catches fire, discolors, and gives off harmful fumes
- **ABS**: gives off cyanide compounds and melts into a mess
- **HDPE** (milk-jug plastic): melts and catches fire
- **PTFE / Teflon**: toxic fluorine fumes
- **Fiberglass, carbon fiber, and coated/epoxy composites**: toxic fumes
- **Chrome-tanned leather**: releases chromium (use **veg-tan** leather only)
- **Metals and reflective surfaces**: a 40W CO2 laser can't cut them, and they can **reflect the beam**

**Usually safe:** plywood (laser-grade), MDF, solid wood, cardboard, paper, **cast acrylic (PMMA)**, veg-tan leather, cotton/felt fabric, cork.

> **Rule of thumb:** If you don't know exactly what it is, **don't cut it.**

---

## 👓 6. Eye Protection

- Wear **laser safety glasses rated for CO2 lasers: 10,600 nm (10.6 µm)**, with a suitable **OD rating (OD 5+ recommended)**.
- Glasses made for green, blue, or diode lasers **do NOT protect** against CO2 wavelengths. Check the label.
- **Never look at the beam** or run the laser with the lid open. The CO2 beam is **invisible**.
- Don't defeat or bypass the lid interlock.

---

## ❄️ 7. Water Cooling / Chiller: Very Important

The laser tube is glass and is cooled by water. Overheating will **shorten the life of the tube or crack it**.

- **Water temperature must NEVER go above 25°C. That is the absolute maximum.**
  - Ideal range: **15–20°C**.
  - If it's getting close to 25°C, **stop and let it cool down.**
- **Never fire the laser without water flowing.** Check that the pump is running and water is moving *before* you start a job.
- **The pump heats the water even when the laser isn't firing.** It adds heat over time.
  **➡️ Turn the pump OFF when you are not actually lasering.** Turn it on just before cutting, and off when you're done.
- Use **distilled water only** (no tap water). Check for air bubbles in the tube.
- Don't cool it too far below room temperature either. **Condensation** can form on the tube and electronics.

---

## 💨 8. Air Assist Pressure (PSI)

Use **low pressure (~7 psi) for engraving** and **high pressure (~25 psi) for cutting**. These are starting points, so adjust with a test piece.

| Job / Material | Recommended PSI | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Engraving** (wood, leather, acrylic, anodized surfaces) | **5–10 psi** (use ~7) | Clears smoke without blowing residue across the surface |
| **Cutting wood, plywood, MDF, cardboard** | **20–30 psi** (use ~25) | Stops flare-ups, clears the cut, and gives cleaner, less charred edges |
| **Cutting cast acrylic** | **5–10 psi** | Low air gives clear, flame-polished edges. High air leaves them frosty |
| **Paper, thin fabric, felt** | **5–10 psi** | High air blows light material around |
| **Safe foams (PE, EVA)** | **10–15 psi** | Controls flare-ups without tearing the foam |

- **Never run with air assist OFF.** Even on low it protects the lens from smoke and debris.
- If you see flames while cutting, **turn the pressure up**. Cutting too slowly can also cause flare-ups.
- Don't go above ~30 psi unless your nozzle and compressor are rated for it.

---

## ✅ 9. Before / During / After Checklist

**Before:**
- [ ] Material is known and **safe** (see above)
- [ ] Laser glasses on (**10,600 nm rated**)
- [ ] Water pump **ON**, water flowing, temperature **below 25°C**
- [ ] Exhaust **ON**, air assist **ON** and set to the right PSI (~7 engrave / ~25 cut)
- [ ] Focus height set for the material
- [ ] Bed clear of scraps; fire extinguisher within reach

**During:**
- [ ] **Stay with the machine** and watch for flames
- [ ] Keep an eye on water temperature
- [ ] Lid closed

**After:**
- [ ] Let the exhaust run ~1 minute before opening the lid
- [ ] Remove parts and scraps
- [ ] **Turn the water pump OFF**
- [ ] Turn off laser, exhaust, and air assist

---

## 🔧 10. General Care

- Keep the **lens and mirrors clean** (lens wipes + isopropyl alcohol). Dirty optics lose power and can crack.
- Clean the **honeycomb bed and debris tray** regularly.
- Run a **test cut on scrap** before cutting good material.
- Start with lower power and more passes rather than max power. It's easier on the tube.
